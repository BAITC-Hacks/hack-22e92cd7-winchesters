# Multilingual (Kazakh / Russian / English) angle — findings and proposals

Scope: make every text- and speech-dependent part of AI Leader ID work equally well in KK/RU/EN, including code-switched and rural Kazakh input, and turn that into demoable features. # Findings

## 1. LLM capability in Kazakh (2025–2026) and the practical scoring risk

**Benchmarks.**
- KazMMLU (ACL 2025, ISSAI): 23,000 MMLU-style questions — 10,969 Kazakh, 12,031 Russian — from real Kazakhstani school/university materials. Best models: GPT-4o 75.7% and DeepSeek V3 75.6% average with English prompt instructions; Llama-3.1-70B 55.2%; Gemma-2-27B 54.0%. Three findings matter for us: (a) "models tend to perform slightly better in Russian than in Kazakh"; (b) "LLMs achieve higher accuracy when prompted in English" even when the question is in Kazakh/Russian, across 0–3-shot; (c) a negation-sensitivity test shows models keying on surface words rather than logic (Llama-3-70B reading literacy 57.1% → 50.0% under negation). Claude and Gemini were not evaluated. [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12829v1]
- Anthropic's own multilingual table (MMLU translated by professional humans) does not list Kazakh or Russian at all. The lowest listed languages: Swahili 91.1% of English performance and Yoruba 79.7% for Sonnet 4.5; Haiku 4.5 collapses to 78.3% / 52.7% on those two. Anthropic explicitly says "Test with any languages relevant to your specific use cases", to state the target language in the system prompt, and to "use native scripts... rather than transliteration". Implication: small models degrade disproportionately on low-resource languages; Kazakh has to be treated as untested until we test it. [source: https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/docs/build-with-claude/multilingual-support]
- Sherkala-Chat 8B paper (Inception/MBZUAI, 2025): on Kazakh MT-Instructions-80 judged by GPT-4o, Sherkala scored 5.99 vs GPT-4o 8.81 (0–10). The "Kazakh-specialised" open models (Sherkala, KazLLM-1.0, Irbis) are far behind frontier models, and Sherkala scored 0.97 on Russian Vicuna-80 — so a Kazakh-tuned open model is not a shortcut for a trilingual product. Useful method to copy: GPT-4o-as-judge validated with a 1,000-sample human check (90.4% agreement on a binary label). [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01493v1]
- Kazakh QA (Information, 2025): GPT-4o 0.869 correctness under ideal retrieval, 0.632 end-to-end; Gemini 2.5-flash also tested. Frontier models lead but are far from ceiling on Kazakh. [source: https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.3390/info16110943?fields=title,abstract,year]
- Qorǵau (Kazakh–Russian bilingual safety eval): Claude had the highest "safe" rate in Kazakh (96.5%), but the authors warn this "likely reflect[s] reduced model comprehension" — "models do not fully understand the meaning of Kazakh attack questions" and answer generically. They also document responses coming back in Russian or English when the instruction was in Kazakh, and different degradation patterns on 500 code-switched KK–RU prompts per model. [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13640]
- UNT (Kazakhstan university-entrance) math study: only the Russian versions were tested; the authors flag the missing Kazakh comparison as a limitation. Zero-shot spread was huge (o1 100%, Claude 60.4%), showing model choice dominates. [source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12477718/]
- I found no published study of rubric-based essay/competency scoring in Kazakh. Nothing to cite; the team would be first, which is itself a demo point.

**Bias direction is not obvious — it can go both ways.**
- Leniency: on professionally translated, semantically identical instruction–response pairs in 23 languages (Russian included, Kazakh not), 8 LLM judges/reward models gave lower-resource languages systematically higher scores; reward-model correlation between resource level and score ρ = −0.81; shifts ~0.4–0.5 points on 1–5 scales. Authors also show per-language thresholding is fragile because 44% of code-switched inputs were misclassified by LID. [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14480]
- Style penalty: identical content rewritten with informal language, grammar errors, or non-native phrasing lost up to 1.90 points on a 10-point scale in essay grading (Llama 3.3 70B; Qwen 2.5 72B similar pattern), effect sizes d = 0.64–4.25; near-zero in math/programming. Explicit "Do NOT penalize grammar/style" instructions did NOT remove the bias — "the bias operates at a representational level deeper than prompt-accessible instruction following". [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18765]
- L1 bias in essay scoring: Gemma-3-27B on TOEFL11 gave European-L1 writers higher within-band scores (+0.55 SD German) and East-Asian-L1 writers lower (−0.34 Korean/Japanese) at the same human score; authors recommend "L1-disaggregated fairness audits rather than relying on overall agreement". [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14605]
- Implicit demographic signals: six LLMs graded essays differently when demographics were implied by conversation history; Llama-70B inflated by +1.57 points; GPT-family most stable. Recommendations: rubric anchoring, demographic masking, model selection, disclosure. [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16993]
- ELL essay scoring (Llama-3.1-8B, PERSUADE 2.0): an implicit penalty pathway driven by ELL-typical text features plus an explicit compensation pathway (+0.22 when ELL status is stated), 4:1 penalty-to-benefit asymmetry. [snippet: https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-29773-0_26]

**Net practical risk for inVision U.** A Kazakh-language applicant is exposed to (i) noisier scores (comprehension loss → generic judgements, fewer verifiable quotes, regression toward "normal"), (ii) possible inflation (low-resource leniency) that would make Kazakh scores less discriminating and hurt strong Kazakh applicants in ranking, and (iii) a manner-of-speech penalty on informal/rural/code-switched phrasing that prompts alone cannot switch off. Which effect dominates for Claude on Kazakh BARS scoring is unknown — nobody has measured it — so it must be measured on a parallel corpus (see corpus section). Metrics: per-language mean shift per indicator; ordinal Krippendorff's alpha / ICC between language versions of the same answer; Spearman rank correlation of candidate rankings across languages; quote-recovery rate (share of cited evidence spans that string-match the source); "generic verdict" rate (share of indicators scored exactly "normal" with no quote).

## 2. Scoring strategy: source language vs translate-then-score vs both

- The multilingual LLM-judge survey (33 papers) says: validate the judge in each target language rather than assuming English validation transfers; human-evaluate at least a subset for low-resource languages; consider non-LLM metrics alongside; separate linguistic from cultural competence. It offers "no systematic guidance on translate-then-judge versus native evaluation". [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02235]
- Evaluation decomposition (2026): generate a shared, language-agnostic criteria set as targeted questions in English; have the LLM answer each criterion on native-language text; combine with a small predictor trained on English labels. Gains of +3.1 to +7.4 balanced-accuracy points across 9 languages vs holistic judging; authors chose English for the criteria because "LLMs exhibit more consistent reasoning and intermediate representations in English", while the content stays native. This is structurally identical to BARS: behavioral indicators = criteria questions. [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18557]
- KazMMLU independently found English instructions + native content beats native instructions. [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12829v1]
- Translationese bias: LLM judges systematically favour machine-translated text over human-authored text, more in low-resource languages — a warning that scoring an MT'd version changes the score for reasons unrelated to the candidate. [snippet: https://awesomepapers.io/llm-papers/papers/2603.10351]
- Information loss in KK→EN translation for our content type: Kazakh evidentiality/converb chains, kinship-based leadership framing ("ауыл", "ағайын", "жамағат" — community/kin structures), and religious-ethical vocabulary do translate, but machine translation flattens register and can turn hedged first-person reports into assertive English, which would inflate "confidence" readings and erase humility markers that a Kazakh reader would score as values-positive. [unverified — expert judgement; this is exactly what the parallel corpus should test]

**Recommendation (pipeline).**
1. Primary score = native-language pass: system prompt and BARS/indicator questions in English; candidate text untouched; per-indicator output = {level ∈ weak/normal/high, evidence quotes verbatim in source language, one-line rationale in the committee's language}. Verify each quote by exact/normalized substring match; unverifiable quote → indicator marked "no evidence", never silently kept.
2. Secondary pass (Stage 2, optional) = same prompt on an LLM translation into English. Not averaged in. Used only to compute a disagreement flag: if any indicator level differs by ≥1 step, the candidate card shows "language-consistency check: review" and both quote sets. This gives the committee a human-in-the-loop hook and gives judges a visible bias-control mechanism.
3. "Cross-lingual consistency" metric to show judges: on the parallel corpus, (a) ordinal Krippendorff's alpha between KK/RU/EN versions per indicator (target ≥ 0.80), (b) mean level shift per language (target |Δ| ≤ 0.15 on the 3-level scale), (c) rank correlation of the full candidate ordering across languages (target Spearman ≥ 0.90), (d) quote-recovery rate per language (target ≥ 0.95). Show as one table on the Fairness page, recomputed by a script.

## 3. Code-switching and language identification

- Kazakh–Russian LID study (2026): 3,076 Telegram/Kaspi/2GIS documents; the key insight is annotation — integrated Russian loanwords stay "Kazakh", only clause-level switches are "mixed", otherwise "almost any Kazakh sentence" gets tagged bilingual. Test macro-F1: FastText 0.709 (mixed recall 0.49), HeLI raw 0.697, HeLI+windows 0.869, char-3gram NB 0.880, Lingua 0.886 (mixed recall 0.988 but precision 0.768 — over-flags mixing), XLM-R LID 0.966. Code, data and weights released. [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00581] Repo: XLM-R LID v2 (ru / kz / mixed) macro-F1 96.56%, FastAPI demo, no explicit license in README. [source: https://github.com/naadgob/KazNLP]
- 100k Kazakhstani movie reviews: code-switch defined as "a multiword segment from another language... typically including the function words or grammatical marking of that language"; isolated borrowings are loanwords. Only 1.11% of reviews were code-switched vs 2.63% Kazakh and 96.27% Russian — in written registers true switching is rarer than the fear suggests, but the loanword problem is everywhere. [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08600]
- GlotLID v3 (fastText, 2,102 language+script labels): kaz_Cyrl F1 0.991, rus_Cyrl 0.992, eng_Latn 0.884 on its own test data; I could not find a kaz_Latn label in the visible list; authors warn "avoid using it on very short sentences". [source: https://github.com/cisnlp/GlotLID] [source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cisnlp/GlotLID/main/languages-v3.md]
- lingua-py supports Kazakh, Russian, English; `detect_multiple_languages_of()` returns contiguous single-language spans (experimental); `compute_language_confidence_values()` gives probabilities. [source: https://github.com/pemistahl/lingua-py]
- Per-language thresholds are fragile: 44% of code-switched inputs misclassified in the language-bias study. [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14480]
- Latin script: the April 2021 alphabet uses ä ö ü ğ ş ñ ū; official phased use 2023–2031 (deadline moved from 2025 to 2031 in Jan 2021). Wikipedia gives no adoption statistics. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakh_alphabets] Informal SMS-era Latin transliterations of Kazakh (and Russian) predate the reform and are idiosyncratic. [snippet: https://voicesoncentralasia.org/kazakhstans-new-latin-alphabet-project-spurs-discussions-online/] How many 2026 applicants will type Latin Kazakh is unknown [unverified]; expect a small minority, mostly informal ad-hoc schemes rather than the official 2021 set. TurkicNLP ships bidirectional Cyrillic↔Latin transliteration for the 2021 alphabet. [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19174v4]
- Cheap deterministic signal: Kazakh-only Cyrillic letters (ә ғ қ ң ө ұ ү h і) are a near-perfect document-level KK-vs-RU discriminator when present [unverified but standard], which is presumably what the current heuristic does; it fails on short, mixed, or Latin text and cannot label spans — that is the misclassification the code note describes.

**How not to penalise code-switching.** The client forbids manner-of-speech penalties. Concretely: (1) the language label must never be an input feature to any scorer or threshold; (2) the scorer prompt must state that mixed Kazakh/Russian/English is expected and carries no weight; (3) evidence quotes are kept verbatim in the original mix; (4) fairness audit disaggregates by {kk, ru, en, mixed} and reports the same consistency metrics; (5) stylometry runs per detected language span, never on the whole mixed text with a single language's thresholds.

## 4. Kazakh / Russian text processing in Python

- Russian: pymorphy3 2.0.6 (released 2025-10-09), Python 3.9–3.14, Russian + Ukrainian, MIT, `parse(word)[0].normal_form`. Mature; 1 hour to integrate. [source: https://pypi.org/project/pymorphy3/]
- Kazakh via Stanza (UD 2.12 kk_ktb): tokenization 99.42, lemma 89.21, UPOS 85.58, UFeats 78.70, LAS 51.38 — vs Russian ru_syntagrus lemma 97.65, UPOS 98.06. Kazakh lemmatization is usable for aggregate statistics (MATTR), not for per-word decisions. [source: https://stanfordnlp.github.io/stanza/performance.html] The underlying UD_Kazakh-KTB is "too small to provide for a standard training-development-test split" (wiki/fiction/news, CC BY-SA 4.0, last release v2.11, Nov 2022). [source: https://github.com/UniversalDependencies/UD_Kazakh-KTB]
- TurkicNLP (2026): `pip install turkicnlp[stanza]`; Kazakh tokenization, POS, lemma, dependency (Stanza and Glot500 backends), morphology via Apertium FST (60% detection / 97% analysis coverage / 69% feature coverage) or neural Glot500 (70% / 100% / 82%), NER, Cyrillic↔Latin transliteration, NLLB-200 MT. Kazakh UPOS 87.9 (Stanza) / 88.8 (Glot500), UAS 67.4 / 71.5. This is the single most useful package: one dependency covers lemmatization, transliteration, and a morphological analyser. [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19174v4]
- Apertium-kaz: HFST morphological transducer + constraint-grammar tagger; the project page is an "early draft" user guide from 2018 focused on adding stems; no coverage or accuracy numbers on the page. [source: https://apertium.github.io/apertium-kaz/] Coverage "around 90%" appears only in a search excerpt. [snippet: https://wiki.apertium.org/wiki/Apertium-kaz]
- kaz-morph (Rust with Python/WASM bindings, MIT): returns lemma + POS + number/possession/case; self-described "early development"; no evaluation, no install docs. Not for production this autumn. [source: https://github.com/1morello/kaz-morph]
- KazMorphCorpus-2025 + hybrid FST/CRF/KazRoBERTa analyser: 150k sentences, 92.3% accuracy; public release of corpus/code not confirmed. [source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12741073/]
- Stopwords: NLTK's Kazakh list was contributed by an Almaty team in 2016 as freely redistributable [source: https://github.com/nltk/nltk_data/issues/51]; stopwords-iso has `kk`. [snippet: https://github.com/stopwords-iso/stopwords-iso]
- CEFR-graded Kazakh lexicon (2026): 4,561 lemma–POS entries A1–C1, publicly released with code. Usable to measure "vocabulary level", but vocabulary level is a schooling/region proxy, so it belongs in the audit, not the score. [source: https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.3390/technologies14060346?fields=title,abstract,year]
- Kazakh–Russian code-switched MT and KazParC (371,902 parallel sentences KK/EN/RU/TR; Tilmash model "on par with... Google Translate and Yandex Translate" on BLEU/chrF) exist if the team wants local translation. [source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19399]

**Should keyword-bank signal extraction be replaced by LLM extraction with verbatim quotes?** Yes, unequivocally. Keyword banks are (a) English-only today, (b) unfixable for Kazakh without morphology — "жетекшілік еттім / жетекші болдым / басқардым / ұйымдастырдым" plus every case/possessive suffix would need a transducer just to match "led" — and (c) a manner-of-speech bias vector by construction (they reward the vocabulary of polished city schools). LLM extraction with span verification is language-agnostic, explainable (the quote is the explanation), and auditable. Keep lemmatizers only for stylometry normalization.

Effort: pymorphy3 (S, 1 h); TurkicNLP install + lemma/transliteration wrappers (S, half a day; check model download size and cold-start for the FastAPI process — load once at module level); LLM extractor with span check replacing all keyword banks (M, 2–3 days including prompt + JSON schema + tests on the 16 synthetic candidates + parallel corpus).

## 5. Stylometry across languages and the AI-detection question

Metric-by-metric for an agglutinative language (Kazakh builds "our-hearts-in" as one token, жүректерімізде, per kaz-morph's own example [source: https://github.com/1morello/kaz-morph]):
- Type-token ratio: length-dependent and morphology-dependent. TTR itself has been proposed as a measure of a language's morphological complexity (Kettunen 2014, Journal of Quantitative Linguistics — abstract not retrievable, title verified via https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1080/09296174.2014.911506?fields=title,abstract,year), which is precisely why raw TTR is not comparable across KK/RU/EN: a Kazakh essay will always look "richer". MATTR (moving window, typically 50–100 tokens) removes the length effect [snippet: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2772766124000740]; the morphology effect needs lemma-level (or stem-level) counting, i.e. Stanza/TurkicNLP lemmas for KK, pymorphy3 for RU, simple lowercase for EN, then per-language standardisation. Verdict: keep, as lemma-level MATTR with per-language z-scores.
- Hapax ratio: same two problems, larger. Verdict: drop or lemma-level only.
- Average word length: mostly a language ID feature in this trio (Kazakh > Russian > English). Verdict: drop from any cross-language comparison; at most per-language z-score.
- Average sentence length and sentence-length variance: comparable in principle, but punctuation habits differ and Kazakh spontaneous writing chains converbs into long sentences [unverified]; sentence splitting must be language-aware (Stanza). Verdict: keep as per-language z-scores.
- Formality / filler ratio: needs hand-built lists per language; filler and discourse-marker lists are register/dialect markers — in Kazakh "яғни", "негізі", "жалпы", in Russian "ну", "вот", "типа", "как бы" — and penalising them is literally penalising manner of speech. Verdict: remove from any score; if kept as descriptive metadata, never as a flag.
- Essay–transcript vocab overlap (Jaccard): ASR-contaminated for Kazakh (27–45% WER planning figure) and surface-form based; two Kazakh texts about the same event can share almost no exact tokens because of suffixes. Verdict: lemma-level, gate for Kazakh transcripts, keep only as a "consistency across sources" indicator with a confidence badge (see 6).

**Deriving thresholds empirically.** Stage 2 gives anonymized historical assessments: compute per-language (kk/ru/en; mixed handled per span) distributions of each retained metric, store mean/SD (or robust median/MAD) as a versioned JSON, express every candidate metric as a z-score/percentile within its language, and flag only |z| > 2 with the percentile shown, not a verdict. Before Oct 3, bootstrap the same JSON from the 16 synthetic candidates + the parallel corpus + public Kazakh/Russian student-style text so the mechanism is demoable, and label it "provisional, to be re-fit on historical data".

**Is AI-text detection for Kazakh feasible?** No, not at acceptable false-positive rates, and it is a bias vector aimed at exactly our cohort:
- GPT detectors flagged more than half of TOEFL essays by non-native writers as AI while being near-perfect on US 8th-grade essays; cause: low perplexity of constrained, textbook-like language. [snippet: https://arxiv.org/html/2304.02819]
- 2026 study of 135,389 non-native academic manuscripts: token-statistics detectors had 99.8–100% false-positive rates, classifier detectors 7.6–93.9%; professional editing moved scores in opposite directions across detector families; "detector scores may indicate linguistic refinement rather than AI generation". [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26710v1]
- Russian: in M4GT-Bench cross-lingual detection, Russian fell in the lowest tier (F1 < 50 for XLM-R). [snippet: https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11175] The AINL-Eval 2025 Russian shared task found systems "struggle significantly" on unseen generators. [source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.09622]
- Kazakh: I found no machine-generated-text detection dataset or benchmark for Kazakh at all. Any Kazakh "AI score" the product shows is therefore uncalibrated by definition.
- The applicant population writes in a second or third language much of the time (Russian-medium school + Kazakh home, or vice versa), which is the exact profile the false positives hit.

Reframe: "Consistency across sources" (essay ↔ video ↔ Feynman chat ↔ interview notes: same events, names, numbers, roles) plus "Specificity" (ATOLA-style: concrete action, own contribution, measurable outcome, named learning) — both are competency-relevant, language-neutral, and produce human-readable evidence. Never output the words "AI-generated" or "authenticity score". A low consistency score triggers an interviewer probe, not a penalty.

[source: https://elevenlabs.io/speech-to-text/kazakh] The current ElevenLabs docs list Kazakh (kaz) for Scribe v2 in the ">5% to ≤10% WER" tier, with smart language detection and `language_probability` in the response. [source: https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/speech-to-text] A per-word `logprob` field was added to the STT response on 2025-05-19. [source: https://elevenlabs.io/docs/changelog/2025/5/19] Scribe v2 handles multiple languages in one file ("automatically detects each language and transcribes it correctly"). [source: https://elevenlabs.io/blog/introducing-scribe-v2] Artificial Analysis' 2.2% WER index for Scribe v2 is English-only datasets and says nothing about Kazakh. [source: https://artificialanalysis.ai/speech-to-text/models/elevenlabs-scribe-v2]

[source: https://aclanthology.org/2026.lrec-1.431/]
- Pipeline paper (Information, 2026): generic Whisper-large-v3 43.20% → 11.88% after Kazakh fine-tuning; SeamlessM4T-v2 9.72%; VAD preprocessing made things worse; LLM post-correction (Gemma-2-9B, Qwen2.5-7B) raised WER by 5.5–7.2 points. So "fix the transcript with an LLM" is not a free lunch. [source: https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.3390/info17070690?fields=title,abstract,year]
- KSC2-fine-tuned Whisper-turbo community models: 9.16% WER on the KSC2 test split [source: https://huggingface.co/abilmansplus/whisper-turbo-ksc2]; a KK+RU LoRA variant: 8.92% KSC2 / 8.95% Golos-crowd, MIT, no timestamps/punctuation, chunk >30 s [source: https://huggingface.co/abilmansplus/whisper-turbo-kaz-rus-v1]; a wav2vec2 KK/RU/mixed CTC model: 12.78% KK / 10.47% RU on FLEURS with KenLM, Apache-2.0, trained partly on a private code-switching corpus [source: https://huggingface.co/alibiserikbay/kazakh-russian-mixed-stt]. KSC2 itself is ~1.2k h, MIT, mixing crowdsourced read speech with TV/radio/senate/podcast speech; the test-split composition is not stated on the card. [source: https://huggingface.co/datasets/issai/Kazakh_Speech_Corpus_2]
- Nobody publishes a WER on teenage spontaneous monologue recorded on a phone in a village. The team's 27–45% planning range for spontaneous Kazakh stands; the 3–10% vendor numbers are read-speech FLEURS.

**Vendors with Kazakh (verified support, not verified quality).** ElevenLabs Scribe v2 (kaz, logprob, language detection) [sources above]; Azure Speech `kk-KZ` for fast transcription only, no post-stream refinement (Russian has all three) [source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/language-support]; Google STT v2 `kk-KZ` on chirp and chirp_2 (asia-southeast1, europe-west4), not chirp_3 [source: https://docs.cloud.google.com/speech-to-text/docs/speech-to-text-supported-languages]; Gemini 3.5 Transcribe lists `kk-KZ` among 85+ languages but word timestamps "degrade transcription accuracy" [source: https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/models/gemini-3.5-transcribe]; AssemblyAI Universal-3.5 Pro $0.21/h and Universal-2 $0.15/h with Kazakh, no WER stated [source: https://www.assemblyai.com/languages/kazakh]; Deepgram Nova-3 — a search excerpt claims Kazakh was added, but the expansion post I opened lists el, lt, lv, ms, sk, ca, et, nl-BE, de-CH, ro and not Kazakh, so treat as unconfirmed [source: https://deepgram.com/learn/deepgram-expands-nova-3-with-10-new-languages-and-multilingual-keyterm-prompting]; Yandex SpeechKit `kk-KZ` incl. mixed KK–RU speech [snippet: https://yandex.cloud/ru-kz/docs/speechkit/] — data-sovereignty and optics of sending minors' interviews to a Russian provider make this a hard sell; ISSAI Söyle: open Whisper fine-tune covering 11 Turkic + UN languages, CC BY 4.0, web demo, and a hosted Soyle app/API on Nazarbayev University infrastructure with a free tier [source: https://issai.nu.edu.kz/soyle-project/] [snippet: https://issai.nu.edu.kz/2024/11/21/soyle-app-issais-first-commercial-ai-product-is-unveiled-at-press-briefing/] — the only in-country option, a plus under Kazakhstan personal-data law and for the AMA calls with inVision U's tech team.

**Transcript-free fluency features are manner-of-speech features.** In a 2026 interpretable L2 speaking assessment, the strongest predictors of human fluency ratings were mean length of run (ρ = +0.749), pause ratio (−0.715), speech rate (+0.676), long-pause rate (−0.580), ASR word confidence (+0.595); per-L1 fairness could not be guaranteed ("cells are too small"). [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26137] These are precisely what a nervous rural 17-year-old speaking in a second language, on a slow phone connection, would score badly on; they measure delivery, not leadership. Verdict: exclude all timing/prosody features from scoring. Allowed uses: an audio-quality gate ("too noisy / too short to transcribe — ask for the written version") and ASR confidence weighting.

**What the product should do.**
1. Make the applicant's own written version of the presentation the canonical text (they already paste a transcript; make it "write what you said, in any language, 150–300 words"). Scoring never depends on ASR.
2. Run ASR (Scribe v2 primary; Söyle or a KSC2 Whisper-turbo as a second opinion in Stage 2) only to (a) surface 3–6 quotable moments with timestamps for the interviewer, (b) compute a written↔spoken consistency indicator, gated: if mean word logprob or `language_probability` is below a threshold, show "transcript confidence low — not used" instead of a number.
3. Confidence highlighting: render the transcript with per-word opacity from logprob; the committee sees exactly what the machine is unsure about. This is the honest, demoable version of "we handle Kazakh".
4. Human-verified transcript only for borderline candidates (committee override flow already exists); 10 minutes of bilingual staff time per borderline case beats any vendor.

## 7. Candidate feedback in natural, respectful Kazakh

- Register: Kazakh has сен (familiar) vs сіз (polite); сіз is expected with strangers, elders, authority figures and in professional settings; using the wrong form "can seem disrespectful or overly familiar". [source: https://preply.com/en/blog/kazakh-pronouns/] For a university writing to a 17-year-old applicant, сіз with 2nd-person polite verb forms (-ңыз/-ңіз, e.g. дамытыңыз) is the safe institutional default; a warm tone comes from content (naming their concrete example, using "біз" for the university's stance), not from switching to сен [unverified — expert judgement; validate in the native-speaker review below]. Never mix сен and сіз in one text — a common LLM failure.
- LLM Kazakh generation failure modes documented in the literature: answering in Russian/English despite Kazakh instructions; degraded comprehension producing generic text [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13640]; open Kazakh models far below GPT-4o [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01493v1]. Expected but unmeasured: Russian calques (word-for-word "Сізге керек..." structures), unnatural pluralisation of nouns after numerals, wrong vowel-harmony variants of suffixes in rare words, and overuse of borrowed abstract nouns where a Kazakh verb phrase is natural [unverified].
- Anthropic guidance: state output language in the system prompt, request "idiomatic speech as if it were a native speaker", use native script. [source: https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/docs/build-with-claude/multilingual-support]
- Cheap quality verification, in order of cost: (1) LID purity lint — every sentence of the feedback must be kaz_Cyrl unless it is inside a quote from the applicant; (2) rule lints — no сен-forms (сен, сенің, саған, -сың/-сің verb endings) when register = сіз, no "AI/model" self-reference, numbers/names identical to the plan; (3) back-translation check — translate the Kazakh feedback to English with a second model and have a third call compare it to the structured feedback plan for missing/added claims (catches meaning drift, not naturalness); (4) native-speaker protocol — 30 feedback texts, 2 native Kazakh speakers (students/teachers, not the team), 5-point scales for naturalness, register appropriateness for a 17-year-old, and factual match to the plan, plus free-text "sounds translated here" span marking; report mean and Krippendorff's alpha; iterate the prompt until naturalness ≥ 4.0 with no register violations. The Sherkala team validated an LLM judge with a human sample the same way. [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01493v1]

# Proposals

## L1. Native-language decomposed BARS scoring with verified quotes
- Category: scoring
- What: One scorer prompt per competency block. System prompt and indicator questions in English (client's BARS text, once available; our proxies now); candidate text passed untouched in its own language/mix. For each behavioral indicator the model returns `{indicator_id, level: weak|normal|high|no_evidence, quotes: [verbatim spans], rationale_ru_or_en}`. Post-process: every quote must substring-match the source after Unicode NFC + whitespace normalization; failing quotes downgrade the indicator to `no_evidence`. Use structured outputs (JSON schema) instead of parsing free text.
- Why: English criteria + native content is the configuration with the best measured cross-lingual stability (KazMMLU; decomposition paper); verbatim quotes are the explainability requirement and remove translation loss; `no_evidence` distinguishes "weak" from "the model could not read it", which protects Kazakh applicants from comprehension noise being recorded as low competence.
- Effort: M; when: before Oct 3 (prototype on our proxies), Stage 2 (re-map to the 9 blocks and real BARS).
- Risks: rationale language must be the committee's (Russian?) — confirm in AMA; models can fabricate near-quotes; the substring check must tolerate the applicant's own typos but not paraphrase (use exact match after normalization, no fuzzy matching).
- Confidence: high that it beats holistic or translate-first; medium on absolute Kazakh quality until L2 runs.
- Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12829v1 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18557 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02235

## L2. Cross-lingual consistency harness and metric
- Category: evaluation
- What: `scripts/xlingual_eval.py` runs the scorer over the parallel corpus (below) and prints/exports: ordinal Krippendorff's alpha per indicator across KK/RU/EN/mixed versions; mean level shift per language; Spearman rank correlation of candidate orderings between languages; quote-recovery rate per language; `no_evidence` rate per language. Surface the table on the dashboard's Fairness page as "Same answer, three languages: does the score move?".
- Why: bias exclusion "verifiable on data" — this is the only direct test that language does not change the score; it also detects the leniency direction (low-resource inflation) that a mean-shift-only check would miss if you only looked for penalties.
- Effort: M (2 days incl. corpus assembly); when: before Oct 3.
- Risks: with ~30 items confidence intervals are wide — report them (bootstrap) rather than a single number; translation quality of the corpus itself confounds results (hence professional/native translation for the core set).
- Confidence: high.
- Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14480 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14605 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02235

## L3. Translate-and-compare disagreement flag
- Category: scoring
- What: Second pass of L1 on an English (or Russian) LLM translation of the answer. Never averaged. If any indicator differs by ≥1 level, the candidate card shows a "language-consistency review" badge with both quote sets; counts feed the Fairness page.
- Why: human-in-the-loop hook that targets exactly the cases where language may have moved the score; cheap insurance against comprehension failures on rare Kazakh vocabulary.
- Effort: S (given L1); when: Stage 2 (cost doubles per candidate; fine at tens of dollars, but add after the core is stable).
- Risks: translationese bias makes the translated pass systematically different — that is why it is a flag, not a score; extra latency.
- Confidence: medium.
- Sources: https://awesomepapers.io/llm-papers/papers/2603.10351 (snippet) ; https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02235

## L4. Code-switch-aware language identification
- Category: language-id
- What: Replace the character-frequency heuristic with a two-level LID: document level via GlotLID v3 (fastText, ~ms latency) with labels collapsed to {kk, ru, en, other}; sentence/span level via lingua-py `detect_multiple_languages_of` or the released XLM-R KK/RU/mixed classifier. Output a `language_profile` = {dominant, spans: [(start, end, lang, conf)], mixed: bool, script: cyrl|latn}. If script = latn and dominant = kk, transliterate to Cyrillic with TurkicNLP before NLP steps (keep original for display). Loanwords are not switches: only spans with function words/grammatical marking of the other language count as mixed (adopt the published guideline).
- Why: stylometry normalization and feedback-language choice need a correct label; a wrong label today silently applies the wrong thresholds; treating "mixed" as a first-class value is how the product proves it does not penalise code-switching (manner of speech, language).
- Effort: S for GlotLID + lingua (half a day); M if hosting the XLM-R model (~1 GB, CPU inference is fine).
- Risks: GlotLID is weak on very short texts (Feynman chat turns) — fall back to the document label for turns < 8 tokens; XLM-R repo has no explicit license — ask the author or use it only offline for the audit; Latin Kazakh in ad-hoc SMS spelling will not transliterate cleanly — detect and route to the LLM untouched (Claude reads it), skip stylometry.
- Confidence: high for kk/ru/en document-level; medium for span-level.
- Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00581 ; https://github.com/naadgob/KazNLP ; https://github.com/cisnlp/GlotLID ; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cisnlp/GlotLID/main/languages-v3.md ; https://github.com/pemistahl/lingua-py ; https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19174v4 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08600

## L5. Replace keyword banks with LLM signal extraction plus span verification
- Category: nlp-tooling
- What: Delete the English keyword banks (initiative, adversity, growth, motivation, leadership-role names) and regex specificity. One extraction call per document returns typed signals `{type: initiative|adversity|role|outcome|learning|application, quote, normalized_summary_en, confidence}`; quotes verified by substring match; the baseline scorer counts verified signals instead of keyword hits. Keep specificity as "does the quote contain a concrete action + own contribution + result" judged by the model, with the quote as evidence.
- Why: the current extractor returns ~zero for Kazakh and Russian essays, which is a direct language penalty (bias-exclusion criterion); it also rewards the register of polished schools (manner of speech). LLM extraction is language-agnostic and the quote is the explanation (explainability criterion).
- Effort: M (2–3 days); when: before Oct 3.
- Risks: cost per candidate rises by one call (fine); confidence calibration is unknown until L2; the extractor must be told that mixed-language text is normal.
- Confidence: high.
- Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18765 (style penalties from surface features) ; https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16993 (rubric anchoring, masking)

## L6. Stylometry v2: lemma-level, per-language, empirically thresholded
- Category: stylometry
- What: Retain MATTR (window 50, lemma-level), sentence-length mean and variance (language-aware splitting), and lemma-level essay↔other-source overlap (gated by transcript confidence). Drop hapax ratio, average word length, and formality/filler ratio from scoring. Lemmatize with pymorphy3 (ru), TurkicNLP/Stanza (kk), lowercase (en). Store per-language distributions as a versioned JSON (`stylometry_norms.json`, provisional now; re-fit on Stage 2 historical data); report percentile within language; flag only |z| > 2.
- Why: raw TTR/hapax/word length encode Kazakh morphology, not writing behaviour (language bias); filler ratios encode dialect and register (manner of speech); hand-set thresholds cannot be defended to the committee; per-language norms make the same flag mean the same thing in every language.
- Effort: M (2 days + Stage 2 re-fit); when: partial before Oct 3 (metric changes + provisional norms), Stage 2 (empirical norms).
- Risks: Kazakh lemma accuracy is ~89%, so MATTR has noise — acceptable for aggregate percentiles; mixed texts must be processed per span (L4).
- Confidence: high on which metrics to drop; medium on the value of what remains.
- Sources: https://stanfordnlp.github.io/stanza/performance.html ; https://pypi.org/project/pymorphy3/ ; https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19174v4 ; https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1080/09296174.2014.911506?fields=title,abstract,year (title only) ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2772766124000740 (snippet)

## L7. Reframe "AI detection / authenticity" as "Source consistency and specificity"
- Category: stylometry
- What: Rename and rewire the panel. Inputs: essay, written presentation, Feynman transcript, video transcript (if confident). Outputs: (a) consistency — same events/roles/numbers across sources, with the matched or contradicting quotes side by side; (b) specificity — per ATOLA element, is there a concrete action, own contribution, measurable outcome, named learning; (c) never an "AI probability". Low consistency → "suggested interview probe", never a score deduction.
- Why: detectors have 50–100% false-positive rates on non-native and edited writing, no Kazakh calibration exists at all, and the false positives land on the rural bilingual cohort the product exists for (language, region, manner of speech). Consistency and specificity are competency-relevant and human-readable, and they map onto the client's own "detect water and socially desirable phrasing".
- Effort: S (mostly prompt + UI copy); when: before Oct 3.
- Risks: the team loses a flashy "AI detector" feature — replace the story with "we refused to build a detector that would have flagged village kids; here is what we built instead" (creativity + bias criteria).
- Confidence: high.
- Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26710v1 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2304.02819 (snippet) ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.09622 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11175 (snippet)

# Recommended minimal parallel evaluation corpus (build in one week)

**Size and content.** 30 answer sets (≈ 30 candidates × 2 texts each: essay-style answer to a leadership question and a 150–300-word written presentation). Sources: the 16 existing synthetic candidates (rewritten to be plausible teen prose), 14 new synthetic personas spanning village/small-town/city, Kazakh-medium and Russian-medium schools, low and high formal achievement. Each set must contain at least: 10 written natively in Kazakh, 10 natively in Russian, 10 natively in English — the native version is the "source", not an English original.

**Versions per item (5).** Source language; two translations into the other languages (professional or native bilingual translator for the 10 Kazakh-source items — this is the set that matters; LLM translation with a native check for the rest); one code-switched variant (KK matrix with Russian clauses for kk-source, RU matrix with Kazakh phrases for ru-source), written by a bilingual person, not generated; one manner-of-speech variant (informal/regional, facts unchanged). Optionally one Latin-script Kazakh variant via TurkicNLP transliteration for 5 items.

**Labels.** Two bilingual raters assign indicator levels on the source version using the proxy BARS (weak/normal/high per indicator), blind to each other; keep both and report agreement. In Stage 2, replace or supplement with anonymized historical assessments and, if permitted, real de-identified answers translated the same way.

**Budget.** Translation: 10 Kazakh items × 2 targets × ~400 words = ~8,000 words; at typical Almaty freelance rates this is well under $200 [unverified]; alternatively two Kazakh-major students for a day. LLM cost for scoring 30 × 5 versions × ~10 calls: a few dollars.

**How to use it.** (1) L2 consistency metrics per language and per indicator, with bootstrap CIs; (2) L12 style-invariance spread; (3) LID accuracy on the mixed and Latin variants; (4) quote-recovery and `no_evidence` rates per language; (5) human–model agreement per language against the two raters (report alpha per language separately — the difference between languages is the finding); (6) regression test in CI: any prompt or model change re-runs the harness and fails if alpha drops below the stored baseline or mean shift exceeds 0.15. Show the resulting table live on Demo Day.

# Things to stop doing

1. English-only keyword banks for initiative/adversity/growth/motivation/leadership roles — they return near-zero for Kazakh and Russian essays, which is a language penalty on the majority of applicants; replace with verified-quote extraction (L5).
2. Hand-set, language-specific stylometry thresholds — indefensible to the committee and wrong for agglutinative Kazakh; move to lemma-level metrics with empirical per-language norms (L6).
3. Presenting an "AI-text / authenticity score" — no Kazakh calibration exists, non-native false-positive rates are 50–100% in the literature, and the false positives land on the bilingual rural cohort; reframe as consistency and specificity (L7).
4. Character-frequency language detection used as a gate — misclassifies code-switched and Latin-script text and silently applies the wrong thresholds; use GlotLID + span-level LID and treat "mixed" as first-class (L4).
5. Any scoring path that depends on a Kazakh ASR transcript (currently `essay_interview_vocab_overlap`, the video "authenticity_match", and Whisper as a fallback) — gate by transcript confidence and make the written version canonical (L8).
6. Whisper (any variant, any reseller) for Kazakh, and whisper-large-v3-turbo zero-shot in particular (81.2% WER on KSC2 zero-shot).
7. Filler/formality ratios, average word length, hapax ratio, speech rate or pause statistics as inputs to any score — each is a manner-of-speech or morphology proxy (L6, L9).
8. Using Haiku-class models for Kazakh or mixed text — Anthropic's own table shows disproportionate low-resource degradation (L13).
9. Grouping the fairness audit by recommendation category — it cannot detect bias; disaggregate by language, script, and (Stage 2) region/school (L10).
10. Silently recording "weak" when the model found nothing — force a `no_evidence` state so comprehension failures on Kazakh text are visible to the committee rather than becoming a low score (L1).
11. Related, outside this angle but language-adjacent: the school-type "starting advantage" table is a demographic proxy that correlates with language of instruction (Kazakh-medium schools are disproportionately rural); it should not be an input to any score and should live only in the audit.
