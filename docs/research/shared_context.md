<hackathon_brief>
Client: inVision U — a fully grant-funded ("100% grants", funded by inDrive / Arsen Tomsky) university in Kazakhstan for future leaders, founders and changemakers. Bachelor's launches 2026 with 5 majors (Digital Media & Marketing; Public Policy & Development; Sociology: Leadership & Innovation; Innovative IT Product Design & Development; Creative Engineering) plus a Foundation year (full-ride for underprivileged students: housing, food, stipend, flights; family status is considered for Foundation).
Hackathon: GovTech Camp, task "AI Leader ID". Our team placed 4th of 5,000+ participants in Stage 1. Today is 2026-09-20. Demo Day at "AI & Digital Bridge 2026" is Oct 1–3 (20 teams advance). Stage 2 "Implementation" runs Oct 5 – Nov 10 (final Demo Day Nov 10, Digitalization Day of Kazakhstan). In Stage 2 the advancing teams get: the extended methodology (question scoring markup, competency weights, cut-off thresholds, full BARS scales), ANONYMIZED HISTORICAL DATA of previous candidate assessments (for training/validation), access to inVision U's technical team (APIs, test environments), weekly 15-min AMA calls.

Task statement: build an AI / data-driven PRE-SELECTION solution for candidates with high leadership potential. It is inserted BEFORE the interview as preliminary scoring, NOT as a replacement for the committee. Requirements: Transparency (clear what signals make up the score), Explainability (human-readable justification for every score, tied to a concrete behavioral indicator), Bias exclusion (background must not lower the score; model must not penalize region, school, language, family income, or manner of speech — "verifiable on data"), Recommendations to the candidate (what to develop, not just pass/fail), Scoring for the committee (candidates comparable to each other, decision defensible), Human-in-the-loop (committee sees the logic, can disagree and override; final decision is theirs). Quote from the deck: "A solution with high AUC that cannot explain a single one of its scores, we cannot put into admissions."

Problems they name: (1) manual labor doesn't scale — 25–30 min interview per candidate + English block + video review; growth in applicants = linear growth in committee hours; (2) different interviewers calibrate differently, same behavioral indicators read differently → comparability suffers; (3) socially desirable answers — candidate says what they expect you want to hear; "pouring water" (vague filler) on a general question is easy; (4) standard tests don't measure this — grades and language certificates don't measure leadership potential, values, motivation; (5) unequal starting conditions — a strong village candidate looks weaker than a "polished" city one; selection must see potential, not background; (6) can't just trust a model — this decides a person's fate; needs explainability and the committee's right to say "no" to the algorithm.

What the model MAY do (per client): preliminary scoring of the application (video presentation + ipsative test results); screening out clearly irrelevant applications before the live interview; HINTS TO THE INTERVIEWER — which competencies to dig deeper for this candidate; calibration alignment — a single scale for all interviewers; DRAFT FEEDBACK to the candidate — what to develop; detection of "water" and socially desirable phrasing in answers. What stays with humans: final admission decision; live interview and observation of behavioral indicators; assessing leadership potential; borderline/contested cases; ownership of methodology (competency list, scales, thresholds); responsibility for rejection.

AS-IS selection process: Step 1 — remote stage: candidate's VIDEO PRESENTATION + a LEADERSHIP/COMPETENCY TEST (ipsative, forced-choice). Step 2 — 25–30 min live INTERVIEW following the ATOLA model, checking several blocks at once (motivation for university, motivation for the major, leadership potential, teamwork, prior real experience, values). Step 3 — English + academic block: interview in English and check of understanding of the chosen major; for Foundation, family status is additionally considered. Final: admissions committee combines test scores and interview ratings into a single decision.
Roles: Applicant (answers with examples from real experience: school, community, work, volunteering, own projects; does NOT know which competency is being tested). Interviewer (leads conversation through all ATOLA blocks, asks clarifying questions if answer is generic, records observed indicators in notes; never names the competency aloud). Admissions committee (combines test + interview, compares candidates on a single scale, decides on grant). Methodology owners (inVision U and "Talent Craft": single competency list, behavioral indicators, rating scale, threshold calibration).
Where AI Leader ID fits: BEFORE interview — preliminary scoring and prioritization. INSIDE interview — hints to interviewer which blocks to dig. AFTER — draft feedback to candidate. Admission decision remains with the committee.

METHODOLOGY (what the assessment stands on):
(01) Competency model KFLA — inVision U's values decomposed into 3–6 behavioral indicators each, each mapped to 2–4 competencies of Lominger / Korn Ferry Leadership Architect. Observable behavior is assessed, not self-description.
(02) Ipsative (forced-choice) test — blocks of 4 statements from different competencies, equalized for social desirability. Candidate picks "most like me" and "least like me". Items built on Item Response Theory. 95 forced-choice questions total (bank is closed until Stage 2). Example item (competency: Teamwork): "When an argument starts in the group, I…" A) help participants hear each other even if opinions differ (+2, high) B) wait for the argument to die down so as not to get involved (0, adaptive) C) try to bring the conversation back to the task (+1, moderate) D) try to defend my opinion, otherwise I'll be "trampled" (−1, low). All four options are worded adequately and socially acceptable, so the "right" answer isn't obvious. Total score interpretation bands: +60…+90 very high; +35…+59 high; +15…+34 moderate/stable; −5…+14 adaptive/situational; −20…−6 low; < −20 avoidance/closedness.
(03) Interview by the ATOLA model — Action ("What exactly did you do? How did you approach it?"), Thinking ("Explain your reasoning; why this approach?"), Outcome ("What was the result? What was the impact then and now?"), Learnings ("What did you understand/learn?"), Application ("Give an example where you applied these learnings in another situation"). Single question structure and single indicator scale for all candidates; levels separated by BARS (behaviorally anchored rating scales). Competency is probed from several sides so there's no room for socially expected answers or "water".
Methodology build pipeline: value mapping → mapping to KFLA/Lominger → forced-choice item design (social-desirability-balanced, IRT) → calibration and scoring, levels per BARS.

NINE competency blocks (each opened via 5 ATOLA questions, rated on 3 levels: weak / normal / high): 1 Motivation for the university (why inVision U and not "a good university nearby"); 2 Motivation for the major (sees link between self, the major, and plans); 3 Leadership abilities (already demonstrated leadership via real actions); 4 Teamwork (a "we" orientation while personally accountable); 5 Values (contribution and ethics, not only personal gain); 6 Prior real experience (projects, work, volunteering, initiatives); 7 Intellect (understand the complex and apply it — not "grades"); 8 Purpose-driven leadership (leadership grounded in meaning and mission); 9 Wounded leadership (leadership arising from lived hard experience — must check that past "wounds" don't distort the leadership style into harshness, control, or a victim position). The deck says: "Purpose-driven and Wounded leadership are the hardest to automate and the most valuable for selection."

Example BARS — Leadership abilities. Question: "Tell about the coolest example where you were a leader: what goal you set, whom you involved, what exactly you did, and what result you got." Probe if generic: "Why was this important to you personally? How did you measure the result? What was your personal contribution as opposed to the team's?" Weak: no concrete situations, general phrases; no initiative, passive role; can't organize others, avoids delegating; difficulties cause confusion, avoids responsibility; hard to describe real result or own contribution. Normal: some leadership examples but limited in scale; can organize a small process in a group; takes responsibility but more out of necessity than initiative; copes with difficulties but without a systematic approach; results exist but role in them is moderate/partial. High: clear concrete examples of leadership actions; shows initiative — launches projects, unites people; can distribute tasks, support others, resolve conflicts; takes responsibility for decisions and consequences, fixes mistakes; shows resilience and finishes even in hard circumstances.
Example BARS — Wounded leadership. Question: "Have there been hard situations or difficult experiences that noticeably influenced you? What did you take from it, and how does it show in your decisions and attitude to people now?" Probe: "What concretely changed in your behavior? Which triggers do you know? How do you track and restrain them?" Weak: unaware of the influence of hard experience or avoids the topic; shows harshness or a need to prove significance; easily slips into victim position, defense, or blame; doesn't use personal experience to support others; examples of help/empathy absent or formal. Normal: aware of some elements of hard experience but not always its overall influence; sometimes uses personal experience to support others; empathy present but unstable; conclusions exist but applied selectively; leadership generally constructive but the past sometimes affects behavior. High: clearly aware of hard experience and its influence on motivation to help others; deep empathy and sensitivity to people's pain; uses experience as a resource — supports, holds, acts sustainably; doesn't slide into harshness, control, or victimhood; gives concrete examples where lived experience became a source of positive change.

Disclosed now vs closed until Stage 2: OPEN — methodology structure (KFLA → ipsative test → ATOLA interview), the 9 competencies, 3-level logic and interpretation scale, sample questions/indicators, the AS-IS process and roles. CLOSED — full bank of 95 forced-choice items with scoring, exact competency weights and cut-offs, anti-examples/"traps" in answer options, anonymized past assessment data. Reason: if the bank becomes public, future cohorts prep "for the test" and validity dies.

Judging criteria for Stage 2: Transparency; Explainability (per-score human-readable rationale tied to a specific behavioral indicator); Bias exclusion (not penalizing region, school, language, family income, manner of speech — verifiable on data); Recommendations to the candidate; Scoring for the committee (comparable, defensible); Human-in-the-loop. Organizers also told us CREATIVITY is a major criterion — but creative ideas must be reasonable and implementable.

<current_product>
Repo: FastAPI (Python 3.12) backend + Next.js 16 / React 19 / Tailwind 4 frontend. Team: 1 developer + 1 product designer. Stage-1 product ("InVision U — AI-Powered Admissions Platform"), tagline "the best way to find a leader is to watch them teach".
Features shipped: (a) 5-step applicant form (personal info, education incl. school type + GPA, essay 200+ words, extracurriculars/projects, video link + pasted transcript, review); (b) "Feynman Teaching Challenge" — candidate teaches a school-level topic (seasons, rain, gravity, money, sleep, teamwork, recycling, cooking) to a Claude-played 10-year-old "Arman" over 4–8 chat exchanges; the AI student intentionally misunderstands once to test patience; then the AI student takes a 3-question quiz answering ONLY from what was taught (knowledge-transfer measure, quiz answers shown transparently); a separate Claude call scores Clarity, Patience, Empathy, Adaptability, quiz_transfer, overall; (c) scoring pipeline: pure-Python "signal extractor" (keyword banks, regex specificity, GPA bands, leadership role names, school-type "starting advantage" table: private 75, international 80, lyceum/gymnasium 60, public 40, village/rural 25, minus 5 per adversity keyword) → rule-based baseline scorer + Claude "AI scorer" on 5 OUR-OWN dimensions (Leadership Potential 25%, Motivation & Values 25%, Growth Trajectory 20% = "delta" current−starting, Academic Strength 15%, Communication 15%); weighted sum → recommend / consider / needs attention (thresholds 70/50); (d) AI-text detection: 7 stylometry metrics (TTR, sentence-length variance, hapax ratio, formality/filler-phrase ratio, avg word length, avg sentence length, essay–interview vocab Jaccard overlap) with language-specific thresholds for KZ/RU/EN + Claude qualitative pass, blended 40/60 into an "authenticity score" with flags; (e) video presentation analysis: transcript (pasted, or Whisper if OPENAI key, else a hardcoded mock) → Claude compares video voice vs essay voice ("authenticity_match"), motivation score, themes, growth signals, concerns; (f) admissions dashboard: ranked grid, "Evaluation Settings" weight sliders with live re-ranking, "Fairness Audit" table (count/avg score/range/avg growth by recommendation category), "Hidden Gem" filter (low formal metrics but high teaching/growth), "Sparse Profile" badge (missing interview/recommendation/extracurriculars shifts weight to essay + teaching), candidate detail panel with dimension scores, explanations, evidence quotes, "AI Insight" (baseline vs AI delta), stylometry metrics, video analysis, Feynman score, committee override per dimension with note (overall recomputes); (g) auth (register/login, committee demo account), PII masking (name → "Candidate c-001", emails/phones regex-masked) before Claude calls; (h) dataset: 16 synthetic candidates across school types, 3 languages; a validation script printing distributions and a fairness check.
Known technical weaknesses (observed in code): all storage in-memory dicts + a JSON file (scores/auth reset on restart); Anthropic calls use the synchronous client inside async routes; model pinned to "claude-sonnet-4-20250514"; JSON parsed from free text (no structured outputs / schema); keyword banks (initiative, adversity, growth, motivation, leadership roles) are ENGLISH-ONLY while essays can be Kazakh/Russian — so signal extraction silently returns near-zero for non-English essays; school_type "starting advantage" table is a hand-coded demographic proxy (a potential bias vector and hard to defend); "Fairness Audit" groups by recommendation category rather than by protected attribute, so it doesn't actually test bias; no evaluation harness / golden set / inter-rater agreement; no calibration to the client's actual competency model (our 5 dimensions ≠ their 9 competencies, no ATOLA, no BARS, no ipsative test ingestion); Feynman topics are generic school science, not tied to leadership competencies; no candidate-facing feedback/recommendations output at all; no interviewer-facing output at all; fixed 70/50 thresholds; Whisper is unusable for Kazakh (31–60% WER on spontaneous Kazakh speech; ElevenLabs Scribe v2 chosen as direction, but even the best systems are ~27% WER on spontaneous Kazakh, so any transcript-dependent metric must be gated for Kazakh).
