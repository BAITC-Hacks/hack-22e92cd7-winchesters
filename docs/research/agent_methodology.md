# Critique of current product vs the client's methodology

- **Wrong construct space.** The product scores 5 home-made dimensions (Leadership Potential, Motivation & Values, Growth Trajectory, Academic Strength, Communication). The client assesses 9 competency blocks (Motivation-university, Motivation-major, Leadership abilities, Teamwork, Values, Prior real experience, Intellect, Purpose-driven leadership, Wounded leadership), each on a 3-level BARS. Nothing the product outputs can be placed on the committee's single scale, so it fails "Scoring for the committee (comparable)" by construction.
- **Scores are not tied to behavioral indicators.** Explainability in the brief means "every score → a concrete behavioral indicator from the methodology". The current explanations are free-text Claude rationales for dimensions that have no indicator list. A psychometrician would call this construct-irrelevant narrative, not evidence.
- **"Academic Strength" (15%) directly contradicts the client.** Problem 4 in the brief: "grades and language certificates don't measure leadership potential". GPA in the score is a validity and fairness liability (rural grading is not comparable to lyceum grading).
- **School-type "starting advantage" table is a hand-coded demographic proxy.** Private=75 … village=25, minus 5 per adversity keyword. Even used "in the candidate's favour" it is scoring on background, not behavior; it is indefensible under "Bias exclusion — verifiable on data", and it is the first thing a hostile reviewer will find. It must go, and "Growth Trajectory = current − starting" must go with it.
- **Fairness Audit does not test fairness.** It groups by recommendation category. A bias check groups by protected/contextual attribute (region, school type, language, income proxy, gender) and compares score distributions and error against a reference (committee ratings). The current table cannot detect the bias the client cares about.
- **Signal extraction is English-only while candidates write Kazakh/Russian.** Keyword banks silently return ~0 for non-English essays, so the rule-based baseline is lower for exactly the population the client wants to protect (rural Kazakh speakers). That is a measurable language bias already in the product.
- **No ipsative test ingestion.** Step 1 of the AS-IS process is video + forced-choice test. The product ignores the test, and therefore cannot do the one cross-source thing a pre-screen can do well: compare a candidate's self-described profile with what they demonstrate.
- **No ATOLA, no BARS, no probes.** The interviewer is a named user in the brief ("hints which competencies to dig deeper"), and the product has zero interviewer-facing output. The client's own probe texts exist and are unused.
- **No candidate feedback output**, although "Recommendations to the candidate" is a judging criterion.
- **"Water"/social-desirability detection is a stylometry-and-AI-text detector**, not a construct. Stylometric thresholds by language are a proxy for writing register, which correlates with school type and urban/rural background (see Alvero et al. below) — a bias vector dressed as authenticity.
- **Feynman Teaching Challenge is orthogonal to the methodology.** Teaching "how rain forms" to a synthetic 10-year-old maps to none of the 9 competencies as the client defines them (Intellect is "understand the complex and apply it", not explain school science). It is creative, but presently unanchored, so it is a liability in a methodology conversation unless re-purposed.
- **No measurement infrastructure.** No golden set, no inter-rater agreement, no calibration, free-text JSON parsing, synchronous client in async routes, in-memory storage. None of the Stage-2 validation the client will expect can be run on this base.
- **Fixed thresholds 70/50** and weights owned by the team, not by the methodology owners (inVision U + Talent Craft own competency list, scales, thresholds per the brief). Weights and cut-offs must be configuration supplied by the client, displayed as such.
- **Transcript-dependent metrics are not gated for Kazakh ASR quality**, so Kazakh speakers get noisier scores; noise is not neutral — it usually regresses toward "weak/no evidence".

# Research findings

## 1. Ipsative / forced-choice tests: scoring, limitations, and a defensible ingestion module

**Classical (ipsative) scoring problems.** Brown & Maydeu-Olivares: in multidimensional forced-choice (MFC) questionnaires "if scored with traditional methodology, MFC instruments produce ipsative data, whereby all individuals have a common total test score. Ipsative scoring distorts individual profiles (it is impossible to achieve all high or all low scale scores), construct validity (covariances between scales must sum to zero), criterion related validity (validity coefficients must sum to zero), and reliability estimates." Their remedy: "IRT models based on an appropriate response process for comparative data, such as Thurstone's Law of Comparative Judgment … the IRT-estimated scores are free from the problems of ipsative data." [source: https://kar.kent.ac.uk/30530/]

**Implication for the client's item.** The example item ("When an argument starts in the group, I…", options +2/+1/0/−1) shows a *scored-option* design (each option carries a level weight for one competency), and the interpretation bands (+60…+90 very high … < −20 avoidance) are summed option weights, i.e. **classical partially-ipsative scoring**, not Thurstonian IRT — even though the brief says items were "built on IRT" (which may refer to item calibration, not the scoring). This matters: (a) totals are bounded and cross-candidate comparability of *profiles* is limited; (b) the interpretation bands are norm-free and their meaning depends on the item bank. A module that *recomputes or re-scales* the test is out of scope and out of ownership (methodology owner = Talent Craft). What is in scope: ingest what the test produces, display the client's own bands, and quantify response quality.

**Faking resistance.** Cao & Drasgow (2019) meta-analysis: "the overall score inflation effect size for FC personality measures is 0.06"; FC scales work best "when statements are balanced for social appeal and scored using normative methods" [source: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:31070382%20AND%20SRC:MED&resultType=core&format=json]. A second meta-analysis (52 studies, N=106,266) found FC inventories resist faking better than single-statement scales; in real applicant samples effects were modest (δ≈0.35 for the most job-relevant trait), quasi-ipsative formats were most resistant (δ=0.49 vs δ=1.27 for pure ipsative), and no format is immune [source: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.732241/full]. So the test is the *least fakeable* input the client has; the essay/video are the most fakeable. Design consequence: use the test as the **prior/anchor** and the narrative sources as **evidence to confirm or contradict** — not as three numbers to add.

**Cross-checking self-report vs demonstrated behavior.** Self–informant convergence in personality is only moderate (r = 0.45 in a roommate-dyad study), and "discrepancies between self-report and informant report were significantly associated with … impression management tendencies" [source: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:26951323%20AND%20SRC:MED&resultType=core&format=json]. In automated video interviews, models trained on *interviewer observations* had "consistent convergent and discriminant validity", whereas models trained on *self-reports* "showed minimal evidence of reliability or validity" [source: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:34110849%20AND%20SRC:MED&resultType=core&format=json]; the same study found verbal behavior dominated predictions and urged caution [source: https://www.siop.org/tip-article/research-brief-of-hickman-et-al-s-2022-automated-video-interview-personality-assessments/]. Conclusion: a discrepancy between test profile and demonstrated evidence is *informative*, but its direction is ambiguous (context, modesty, faking, or measurement error), so it should be surfaced as a **flag with a probe**, never as a penalty or an added score.

**What a credible module looks like** (detail in P3): inputs = per-competency test scores (or item-level responses if the client API exposes them) + client's bands; outputs = (i) profile card on the client's own scale, (ii) response-quality indicators (missing items, time-per-block if available, "most/least" pattern flags such as always choosing the same option position; these are standard data-quality checks [unverified as to specific cut-offs]), (iii) a claimed-vs-demonstrated matrix per competency: test band × narrative evidence status ∈ {demonstrated, claimed-only, absent, contradicted}, (iv) probes for the interviewer for every cell that is "high test / absent evidence" or "low test / strong evidence".

## 2. LLM scoring against behaviorally anchored rubrics: reliability, failure modes, designs that help

**Agreement levels.** Rubric scoring of constructed responses by GPT-4o: "human-AI agreement on total scores was comparable to human inter-rater reliability and was highest for high- and low-performing responses, but declined for mid-level responses involving partial or ambiguous reasoning … A more fine-grained, checklist-based rubric improved consistency relative to holistic scoring … reliable AI-assisted scoring depends primarily on clear, well-structured rubrics, while prompting format plays a secondary role and temperature has relatively limited impact." [source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12227]. Essay scoring: strong open-weight models reach QWK ≈ 0.6 with humans on holistic scores, but analytic (trait-level) scoring is worse and shows "large and stable negative directional bias" on grammar/conventions traits; the authors recommend a "bias-correction-first deployment strategy": estimate systematic offsets on a small human-labeled set and correct them [source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00259]. Evidence-first pipelines: converting rubrics into locked checklists, requiring extractive evidence spans before any score, then calibrating to human score distributions raised QWK from 0.46 to 0.71 (ASAP 2.0) and from 0.24 to 0.53 (DREsS EFL essays) versus direct holistic scoring [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08654]. Calibrating rubric-question outputs to human judges with a small model cut error ~2× versus uncalibrated LLM judgments [source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00274].

**Failure modes.** A 12-bias taxonomy for LLM judges (position, verbosity, bandwagon, distraction, fallacy-oversight, authority, sentiment, self-enhancement, diversity/demographic, etc.); "Fallacy-Oversight" had the lowest robustness (0.57–0.83), position and diversity biases were also weak (≈0.66–0.87); no model was reliable across all biases [source: https://llm-judge-bias.github.io/]. A 21-model study found kappa deflation of 33–41 pp between exact-match and Cohen's kappa, judge rankings shifting up to 14 places across benchmarks, and judges with test-retest reliability >0.95 *and* severe position bias — "reliability without validity" [source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19544]. Debiasing works but is model-specific: across 5 judges and 3 benchmarks, the best debiasing configuration reached 71.0% agreement, kappa=0.549, with +4.5 to +11.5 pp gains that survive Holm-Bonferroni [source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23178]. Pairwise judgments are easier for models but need a transfer step to produce absolute scores [source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02069].

**Language fairness.** An LLM essay scorer showed a systematic L1-linked offset within every proficiency band (European-L1 writers +0.21 to +0.33 points over East-Asian-L1 writers; German +0.55 SD, Korean/Japanese −0.34), only visible with band-stratified analysis; authors call for L1-disaggregated audits as standard [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14605]. LLM knowledge in Kazakh lags Russian: GPT-4o 76.6% and DeepSeek V3 76.9% average on KazMMLU, best open models ~57%; "models tend to perform slightly better in Russian than in Kazakh", and the Language category was the hardest [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12829]. A decomposition approach (a language-agnostic "Universal Criteria Set" as an interpretable intermediate layer) transferred judge quality across languages without target-language labels [source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18557] — this is exactly the structure of a BARS-indicator checklist.

**Designs that improve reliability (synthesis):** (1) indicator-level checklist (0/1/2) with mandatory extractive evidence spans *before* any level judgment; (2) the client's own BARS text as the rubric, verbatim, per competency; (3) abstain / "no evidence" as a first-class output; (4) k independent samples with median aggregation and a disagreement flag (the physics paper found temperature secondary, so k>1 buys robustness against sampling noise more than accuracy); (5) length normalization and explicit "do not reward length or eloquence" instructions plus a length-bias audit; (6) pairwise comparison only for calibration (anchor exemplars), not for candidate ranking, and with order randomization; (7) small human-labeled bias-estimation set to estimate and subtract per-competency offsets (bias-correction-first); (8) language-disaggregated audit by band.

## 3. ATOLA mapping, "water" detection, impression management, and probe generation

**Structured interview science.** Higher structure yields "higher levels of validity, rater reliability, rater agreement, and less adverse impact"; OPM prescribes standardized questions, pre-defined probe/follow-up questions, proficiency benchmarks, note-taking, and panels where "each member rates applicant responses individually and then participates in a group discussion to resolve significant scoring discrepancies" [source: https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/assessment-and-selection/other-assessment-methods/structured-interviews/]. Levashina et al. (2014) is the canonical review of the 15 structure components, including probing and BARS [unverified — full text not opened; only the title page via https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1111/peps.12052].

**Faking / impression management.** "The vast majority of applicants use faking tactics in interviews but applicants also vary in the extent to which they engage in faking … faking can potentially change their rank order" [source: https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1111/ijsa.12280]. "Observers tend to be quite poor at identifying and distinguishing honest and deceptive IM"; the one contextual lever with evidence is that "identification warnings against faking can reduce deceptive IM use" [source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5532427/]. Crucially, NLP deception detection failed in a controlled test: open- and closed-vocabulary NLP approaches "did not successfully predict deceptive IM", and "organizations should proceed with caution when deciding to use NLP techniques to predict deceptive IM" [source: https://digitalcommons.odu.edu/psychology_etds/70/]. Design consequence: **do not build a lie detector.** Build a *verifiability and specificity* layer (what can the interviewer check?) and a *coverage* layer (which ATOLA components are missing?), and let the human probe.

**Linguistic proxies to use with care.** Pronoun use tracks social hierarchy (higher status → fewer first-person singular, more plural/second-person) [unverified — abstract not retrievable; search-snippet only, Kacewicz et al. 2014]. Essay content and style "have stronger relationships to household income than do SAT scores and strongly predict SAT scores" (240k UC essays) [source: https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1126/sciadv.abi9031]. So "I vs we" ratios, formality, vocabulary richness, and sentence complexity are **class- and register-laden**; using them as score inputs imports background bias. They may be shown to the interviewer as neutral descriptive facts or used only in *within-answer* structural checks (e.g. "no first-person action verb in the Action segment"), never as competency scores.

**What "water" is, operationally.** Mapping the client's own BARS "Weak" anchors: "no concrete situations, general phrases", "hard to describe real result or own contribution", "passive role". These are checkable indicator absences, not deception: (a) *no situated episode* (no time/place/actors), (b) *no first-person action* (Action segment lacks agentive verbs with "I"), (c) *no reasoning* (Thinking absent), (d) *no outcome or unmeasurable outcome*, (e) *no learning*, (f) *no transfer example* (Application absent), (g) *dispositional instead of behavioral statements* ("I am a natural leader" vs "I organized…"). Each is an ATOLA-component presence/quality judgment with an evidence span, which is exactly what evidence-first LLM rubric scoring does well (see §2).

**Probe generation.** The client already specifies methodology-consistent probes per competency ("Why was this important to you personally? How did you measure the result? What was your personal contribution as opposed to the team's?"). The defensible design is *selection* of the client's pre-approved probes, driven by which ATOLA component/indicator is missing or contradictory, plus optional LLM-drafted paraphrase in the candidate's language — with the source probe shown. This matches the OPM "pre-defined probe" component and keeps the methodology owner in control.

## 4. Calibration between interviewers

Frame-of-reference (FOR) training "is an effective method of improving rating accuracy"; differential accuracy and behavioural accuracy improve most, and gains persist at two-week follow-up [source: https://researchconnect.suny.edu/en/publications/rater-training-revisited-an-updated-meta-analytic-review-of-frame/]. FOR training's core is a shared performance schema built from anchored example behaviors per level — precisely what a "calibration pack" is. Mechanically combining ratings beats holistic combination: "consistent and substantial loss of validity when data were combined holistically — even by experts … In predicting job performance, the difference … translated into an improvement in prediction of more than 50%" [source: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:24041118%20AND%20SRC:MED&resultType=core&format=json]. For drift: "rater severity is known to change over time", especially when "each rater grades many examinees over a period of several hours or several days"; many-facet Rasch models estimate rater severity as a separate facet; fully crossed designs need fewer raters, sparse designs with 2–3 raters per response need more examinees [source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10615980/]. Reliability-of-coding practice in narrative research: double-code ≥20% and report ICC/kappa, treat reliability as an evolving process [source: https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1177/2167696815587648]. Software support that follows from this: (a) anchor exemplars per competency × level (FOR content), (b) a per-interviewer severity/leniency offset vs consensus and drift-over-session plot (MFRM-lite; a full MFRM needs overlapping raters per candidate, which the AMA should confirm), (c) automatic double-scoring of a 20% sample with the LLM as a *second reader* whose disagreements trigger panel discussion — not overrides.

## 5. Wounded and purpose-driven leadership: narrative-identity science and ethical limits

Meaning-making in turning-point narratives is codable: McLean & Pratt coded "meaning-making sophistication, event types, and emotional tone"; sophisticated meaning-making related to mature identity status, generativity, optimism and "redemptive story patterns", and negatively to achievement-centered stories [source: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=AUTH:%22McLean%20KC%22%20AND%20TITLE:%22turning%20point%22%20AND%20PUB_YEAR:2006&resultType=core&format=json]. The commonly used 0–3 meaning-making scale (0 none, 1 lesson, 2 vague insight, 3 elaborated insight) with ICC ≈ 0.73 is reported in the narrative literature [unverified — search snippet; not opened]. McAdams' redemption/contamination sequences, agency, communion, and exploratory processing are the standard narrative-identity codes [unverified — the McAdams & McLean 2013 PDF returned 503]. Reflective functioning (mentalizing) has an adolescent interview-coded scale and a 13-item youth questionnaire (RFQY-13; ω = 0.84/0.80), but interview-based RF coding "requires extensive training, time, and resources", and its use requires informed consent with parental authorization for younger adolescents [source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10613668/].

The critical caveat: "illusory posttraumatic growth is common, but genuine posttraumatic growth is rare"; self-reports of PTG are a mix of genuine and illusory growth [source: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:37331153%20AND%20SRC:MED&resultType=core&format=json]. So a narrative that *sounds* redemptive is weak evidence of growth; the client's own BARS is wiser than a redemption detector because it asks for **behavioral consequences** ("what concretely changed in your behavior? which triggers do you know? how do you track and restrain them?") and for **use of the experience as a resource for others**. Those are checkable behavioral indicators; "growth" is not.

**Adaptable into an explainable indicator rubric (machine-assisted, human-decided):** presence of a specific hard episode (yes/no; no severity rating); awareness statement linking experience → current behavior (0–3 elaboration); at least one concrete behavioral change with example; at least one instance of using the experience to support another person; presence of Learnings and Application ATOLA components; mission statement linked to concrete action (purpose-driven); for purpose-driven: is the "why" tied to others/contribution (communion) and to a sustained pattern of action over time.

**Must remain human-only:** any judgment about victim position, harshness, control, blame, or "distortion of leadership style" (these are clinical-adjacent inferences about a minor's psychology); any estimate of trauma severity; any decision to lower a score because of *how* a hard experience is narrated. The system may only flag "BARS-Weak indicators present: e.g. blame language without reflection — verify in interview" with the exact span, routed to a human. The application UI must not solicit trauma disclosure (the pressure to produce a "trauma essay" is documented as harmful [unverified — opinion sources only]); the interviewer asks the wounded-leadership question live, as the methodology already specifies.

## 6. Minimum credible Stage-2 validation in five weeks

The AERA/APA/NCME framing for AI scores: reliability ("precision of measurement"), validity ("the degree to which empirical evidence and theory support the intended interpretation"), fairness ("reflects the same construct(s) for all test takers"); evidence via human–machine agreement, robustness to construct-irrelevant perturbation, XAI checks that construct-relevant features drive predictions, and subgroup prediction invariance via mean signed error, with no universal thresholds [source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0354680]. Legal-style adverse impact: "A selection rate for any … group which is less than four-fifths (4/5) (or eighty percent) of the rate for the group with the highest rate will generally be regarded … as evidence of adverse impact", with an explicit caveat that small numbers lacking statistical reliability may not constitute adverse impact [source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/1607.4]. LLM-specific: kappa not raw agreement, position/verbosity audits, test-retest [source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19544]; band-stratified L1/language audits [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14605]; bias-correction-first with small labeled sets — lower-order biases detectable with very small validation sets [source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00259].

Feasible plan (detail in P12): per-competency QWK/ICC vs committee level (3-level ordinal), Spearman ρ vs committee composite, AUC/precision at the client's screen-out cut-off, calibration plot of predicted band vs observed band, ablations (test only / narrative only / both; with vs without LLM; language-blind vs full), subgroup mean-signed-error and four-fifths on "recommend" rate with Fisher's exact where cells are small, perturbation tests (translate KZ↔RU, remove school/region cues, shorten/lengthen), test-retest of the LLM scorer, and a one-page pre-registered analysis plan before touching the data.

# Proposals

## P1. Re-anchor the scoring schema on the 9 competencies × 3 BARS levels with an indicator-level evidence ledger
- Category: methodology / improve existing
- What: Replace the 5 dimensions with a `Competency` enum of the 9 blocks, each carrying the client's BARS text (Weak/Normal/High) decomposed into 3–6 indicator checklist items (from the brief now; from the full scales in Stage 2). Every scoring call returns, per competency, an `evidence_ledger`: list of `{indicator_id, status ∈ {present, absent, contradicted, not_assessable}, evidence_span (verbatim quote + source: essay/video/test), confidence}`, then a `level ∈ {weak, normal, high, no_evidence}` derived by deterministic rules from the ledger (e.g. High requires ≥N High-indicators present and no Weak-indicator contradiction), not by the model's gestalt. Store the ledger; the UI renders indicator → quote → level. Weights and cut-offs become a config object labeled "owned by inVision U / Talent Craft".
- Why / criteria served: Transparency and Explainability ("every score tied to a concrete behavioral indicator") become structural rather than prose; Committee scoring becomes comparable because everyone is placed on the client's own scale; Human-in-the-loop improves because overrides happen at indicator level. Evidence-first checklists beat holistic scoring in human agreement (§2).
- Methodology mapping: all 9 competencies; BARS 3 levels; feeds ATOLA components (P2).
- Effort: M, before Oct 3 (with brief-derived indicators), refresh in Stage 2 with full scales.
- Risks: Indicators drafted from the brief may diverge from the client's; label them "provisional" and swap in Stage 2. Level derivation rules must be visible in UI to avoid "hidden algorithm".
- Confidence: high
- Sources: [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08654] [source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12227] [source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18557]

## P2. ATOLA component mapper + "water" (specificity/coverage) index
- Category: new feature
- What: For each narrative source (essay, video transcript, later interview notes) run a segmentation call that labels spans as A/T/O/L/A or `none`, then computes per competency a coverage vector (which of the 5 components have at least one specific span) and per-component quality flags drawn from the BARS Weak anchors: `no_situated_episode`, `no_first_person_action`, `no_reasoning`, `outcome_unmeasurable`, `no_learning`, `no_transfer_example`, `dispositional_not_behavioral`. Output a "water index" = share of claims without any ATOLA grounding, shown as a list of the ungrounded claims (not a single number). No penalty is applied by the water index itself; it changes evidence `status` to `claimed_only`, which caps the level at Normal unless corroborated.
- Why: Directly addresses client problems 3 (water, socially desirable answers) using their own construct (ATOLA), rather than a deception detector that the literature says does not work. Explainable: every flag is a missing component with the span or its absence. Feeds P4 probes and P10 feedback.
- Methodology mapping: ATOLA (all 5 stages); BARS Weak anchors for competencies 3, 4, 6, 8, 9 especially.
- Effort: M, before Oct 3 (essay + transcript), Stage 2 for interview notes.
- Risks: Kazakh ASR noise creates false "absent" flags → gate by ASR confidence (P14). Short answers are not "water"; normalize by prompt, not by length.
- Confidence: high
- Sources: [source: https://digitalcommons.odu.edu/psychology_etds/70/] [source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5532427/] [source: https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/assessment-and-selection/other-assessment-methods/structured-interviews/]

## P3. Ipsative test ingestion, interpretation, response-quality checks, and claimed-vs-demonstrated matrix
- Category: new feature
- What: Define an ingestion schema now (`test_result: {competency_scores: {competency_id: int}, bands (client's), item_responses?: [{block_id, most, least, rt_ms?}], completed_at}`), with a mock generator for the demo. Module outputs: (i) profile card using the client's interpretation bands verbatim; (ii) response-quality flags: incomplete blocks, position-pattern (same option slot chosen as "most" in > X% of blocks), implausibly fast blocks if timing exists, and — Stage 2 only, if item-level data arrive — an intra-person consistency check (agreement between blocks that share a competency); (iii) a `claimed_vs_demonstrated` matrix: rows = 9 competencies, columns = test band × narrative evidence status from P1; cells "high band / absent evidence" and "low band / strong evidence" become interviewer probes, never score adjustments. Never sum test and narrative scores; present side by side, with a committee-owned rule for how the final decision combines them (P13).
- Why: Uses the least fakeable input as an anchor; discrepancy surfacing is what multi-source assessment is for, while respecting that discrepancy direction is ambiguous (§1). Serves Committee scoring (comparable), Human-in-the-loop (probe, don't punish), Transparency.
- Methodology mapping: forced-choice test (95 items, bands), all 9 competencies; ATOLA probes.
- Effort: S for schema + card + matrix before Oct 3; M in Stage 2 for item-level quality checks.
- Risks: Do not re-score or re-band; scoring belongs to Talent Craft. If the client's totals are classical ipsative sums, warn in documentation that profile comparisons across people are limited (Brown & Maydeu-Olivares) and offer, as an optional Stage-2 research note, a Thurstonian-IRT refit if they share item-level data — clearly marked as advisory.
- Confidence: high for ingestion/matrix; low for any IRT refit being accepted.
- Sources: [source: https://kar.kent.ac.uk/30530/] [source: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.732241/full] [source: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:26951323%20AND%20SRC:MED&resultType=core&format=json]

## P4. Interviewer "dig-deeper card" with pre-approved ATOLA probes
- Category: new feature
- What: A one-page, printable/mobile view per candidate: for each of the 9 competencies show evidence status (demonstrated / claimed only / absent / contradicted), the strongest quote, the missing ATOLA components, the test band, and 1–3 probes. Probes are *selected* from a probe bank seeded with the client's own probe texts (e.g., "How did you measure the result? What was your personal contribution as opposed to the team's?") keyed by competency × missing component; the LLM may paraphrase into the candidate's language but the canonical probe is shown. Includes a "do not name the competency aloud" reminder and a space for indicator tick-marks (note-taking support). Estimated interview time saving is shown as "components already evidenced" so the interviewer can allocate 25–30 minutes.
- Why: The brief explicitly allows "hints to the interviewer — which competencies to dig deeper". Pre-defined probes and note-taking are structure components tied to validity and reduced adverse impact. Serves Human-in-the-loop and problem 1 (time).
- Methodology mapping: ATOLA stages; all 9 competencies; BARS indicators as tick-list.
- Effort: S–M, before Oct 3.
- Risks: Interviewer anchoring on the AI's evidence status (confirmation bias). Mitigate: show "absent" as "not yet evidenced in written materials" and hide the level until after the interviewer records their own rating (sequential unmasking).
- Confidence: high

## P5. Debiased, evidence-first LLM scoring pipeline (engineering contract)
- Category: improve existing
- What: (1) Structured outputs (JSON schema / tool-use) for the ledger — no free-text parsing. (2) Two-stage calls: extraction (spans → indicator status) then level derivation in code; the model never outputs a number first. (3) Rubric = client's BARS verbatim, per competency, with 2–3 anchor exemplars per level (P8). (4) `k=3` independent samples on the extraction stage; indicator status by majority; disagreement → `uncertain` flag surfaced in UI. (5) Prompt instructs: do not reward length, eloquence, register, or vocabulary; assess only presence of behavior; output `no_evidence` when absent. (6) PII and background masking extended: strip school name/type, city/village, family income cues from the text seen by the scorer (keep them for the audit table only). (7) Score in the candidate's language; log language; run cross-language consistency test (P14). (8) Small labeled bias-estimation set (Stage 2) to estimate per-competency mean signed offsets and subtract them. (9) Async Anthropic client, pinned current model, temperature low, prompt caching for the rubric.
- Why: Each element maps to a documented failure mode (verbosity, position, leniency, language offset) and to the designs that improved human agreement (§2). Serves Explainability, Bias exclusion, Transparency.
- Methodology mapping: all competencies; BARS levels.
- Effort: M, before Oct 3 (items 1–7), Stage 2 (item 8).
- Risks: Cost ×3 for k=3 — use Haiku 4.5 for extraction, a stronger model only for uncertain cases. Masking background cues can remove legitimate context for competency 6 (prior experience) — mask identity of the school, not the fact "I organized a club at my school".
- Confidence: high
- Sources: [source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23178] [source: https://llm-judge-bias.github.io/] [source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19544] [source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00259] [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08654]

## P6. A bias audit that actually tests bias (attribute-grouped, band-stratified, perturbation-based)
- Category: validation/infra (replaces current "Fairness Audit")
- What: Audit page grouped by contextual attribute: region/oblast, urban vs rural, school type, application language, Foundation (income proxy), gender. For each: n, mean level per competency, "recommend" rate with four-fifths ratio against the highest group and Fisher's exact p when cells < 5, and — once committee ratings exist — mean signed error of the model vs committee *within committee band* (band-stratified so composition differences do not masquerade as bias). Before Oct 3 (no historical data): **perturbation tests** on the synthetic set — translate each essay KZ↔RU, swap village↔city and school-type cues, add/remove code-switching, shorten by 30% — and report the score delta distribution; a delta > 1 indicator status on any competency fails the build. Publish the audit method as a one-page "how we check bias" for the committee.
- Why: The judging criterion is "bias exclusion — verifiable on data". Attribute-grouped and band-stratified analysis is the method the LLM-fairness literature converged on; the four-fifths rule with small-sample caveat is the standard adverse-impact heuristic.
- Methodology mapping: all competencies; the recommend/consider cut-off.
- Effort: M; perturbation suite before Oct 3, historical audit in Stage 2.
- Risks: Small n per region → wide intervals; report intervals, do not over-claim. Some attributes (income) may only be available for Foundation applicants.
- Confidence: high
- Sources: [source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/1607.4] [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14605] [source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0354680]

## P15. Claim ledger and cross-source consistency (honest vs deceptive IM, operationalized as verifiability)
- Category: new feature
